= Theory-of-mind contract v1 conformance fixtures

These fixtures are the cross-repository currency for the rule that a model
is compared with a mind, not with the world (CAC-KERNEL §9, "Models are
compared with minds"; ADR-0028). A reader that shows whether an agent's model of another agent is
right, such as the UMS explain lens, proves conformance against these bytes.
No Enaction source is needed to do so: reimplement the rule from CAC-KERNEL
and derive each `expected.json`.

Every document is an `enaction-runner-output/1` envelope (CAC-KERNEL §9). No
new contract, document or field is involved.

== The cases

Two agents, and a third in `second-order`. `guard-0` holds a model of
`contact-21`: does contact-21 know where guard-0 is (`guard-0 at cell-14`)?
The second proposition, `contact-21 sees guard-0`, is the cue that
guard-0's evidence is about. In every accepted case guard-0 really is at
cell-14, so a reader that compared a model with world truth would get most
of the cases wrong.

Each model has a *timeline*: its verdict from its `valid_from` to the tick
where it is superseded (`until`), or to the end of the package.

* `accepted/true-concealment`: guard-0 thinks contact-21 does not know
  (belief 1, rejected). contact-21 states no belief about it, which means
  that it does not hold it. Belief 1 *agrees* from tick 2.
* `accepted/caught`: contact-21 does not know (1) and guard-0 is right (2).
  On tick 5 contact-21 comes to know (3 supersedes 1) and guard-0 sees the
  gaze (4 supersedes 2). Belief 2 *agrees* from tick 2 until tick 5. Belief
  4 *agrees* from tick 5. Each timeline has one segment.
* `accepted/false-exposure`: contact-21 knows (1) and guard-0 knows that it
  knows (2). On tick 6 contact-21 loses track (3, unknown, supersedes 1).
  guard-0 states its model once and never restates it. Belief 2 *agrees*
  from tick 2 and *diverges* from tick 6: it is *stale-divergent*.
* `accepted/false-exposure-restated`: the same, but the producer restates
  guard-0's model on tick 6 (4 supersedes 2). Belief 2 *agrees* from tick 2
  until tick 6. Belief 4 *diverges* from tick 6. Neither is stale-divergent,
  because neither agreed and then diverged.
* `accepted/false-concealment`: contact-21 knows (1). guard-0 had no cue and
  thinks that it is hidden (2): belief 2 *diverges* from tick 3.
* `accepted/no-claim`: contact-21 knows (1). guard-0's model has status
  unknown (2). It makes no claim: *no-claim* from tick 3.
* `accepted/second-order`: order 2, under this case's own
  `policies.json` with `depth.max_order` of 2. contact-22 knows (1).
  contact-21 thinks it does not (2, *diverges*). guard-0 thinks it does (3,
  *agrees*). guard-0 thinks contact-21 thinks it does not (4,
  `holders = ["guard-0", "contact-21"]`). Read outermost first, as the
  contract reads it, belief 4 is compared with belief 2
  (`holders = ["contact-21"]`), and *agrees*. Read the other way round, it
  would be compared with belief 3 (`holders = ["guard-0"]`) and *diverge*.
  A reader that passes this case reads the path as the contract does.
* `rejected/nesting-bounded`: guard-0's model of contact-21's model of
  guard-0, of order 2, under the corpus depth of 1. Refused by the existing
  `nesting-bounded` rule with path
  `package.beliefs[belief_id=1].holders`.
* `rejected/belief-key-chained`: the `false-exposure` story with its link
  dropped. contact-21 knows (1); on tick 6 it loses track (2, unknown), but
  2 supersedes nothing. Two own beliefs of one key with no contradiction
  record are a silent overwrite (CAC-KERNEL §3 invariant 4, ADR-0029),
  refused with path `package.beliefs[belief_id=2].supersedes`.
* `rejected/supersession-monotone`: 2 supersedes 1 but starts on tick 2,
  before 1 starts on tick 6. A contradiction record never runs backwards
  (ADR-0029), refused with path `package.beliefs[belief_id=2].supersedes`.

== Layout

* `policies.json`: the caller's `enaction-policies/1` document, with
  `depth.max_order` of 1. Its status policy is that of `conformance/mind/v1`.
  A case uses it unless it has its own.
* `accepted/second-order/policies.json`: the same status policy, with
  `depth.max_order` of 2. Under the corpus file, this case is refused as
  `nesting-bounded`.
* `accepted/<case>/runner-output.json`: a valid envelope, in canonical bytes.
  Generated from the contract types by
  `crates/enaction-contracts/tests/tom.rs`.
* `accepted/<case>/expected.json`: every model in the package, by belief id
  in ascending order, with its `timeline` (segments of `from_tick` and
  `verdict`, where `verdict` is `agrees`, `diverges` or `no-claim`), its
  `until` when it is superseded, and `stale_divergent`. Written by hand,
  never generated.
* `rejected/<rule>/runner-output.json`: refused with exactly that rule.
* `SHA256SUMS`: SHA-256 of every other file, sorted by path.

== Use

[source,bash]
----
cd conformance/tom/v1 && sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS
enaction-validate --policies policies.json \
  < accepted/false-exposure/runner-output.json      # exit 0
enaction-validate --policies policies.json \
  < rejected/nesting-bounded/runner-output.json     # exit 1, nesting-bounded
enaction-validate --policies policies.json \
  < rejected/belief-key-chained/runner-output.json  # exit 1, belief-key-chained
enaction-validate --policies accepted/second-order/policies.json \
  < accepted/second-order/runner-output.json        # exit 0
----

`enaction-validate` validates the envelope. It does not print the timelines;
`enaction_contracts::tom::model_timelines` and `stale_divergence` derive
them.

The fixtures are design examples for the four cases of
`docs/proposals/WEEVIL-SLICE-2-IT-MODELS-YOU.adoc`. They are not output of a
real host. No host emits models yet.
