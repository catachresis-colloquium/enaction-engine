= Architecture Decision Record: 0025-independence-by-formalism

Date: 2026-11-17

== Status

Proposed. The number was reserved by step 8 of
`docs/proposals/ULTRACODE-COMPLETION-PLAN.adoc`. The owner ruled the route
on 2026-11-17, by selector, and asked for an experiment before the larger
work. Section <<experiment>> reports it.

Amends ADR-0017 (trigger T1) and replaces part (2) of the plan's Q4 ruling
of 2026-11-05.

== Context

ADR-0017 lets the kernel reducer be designed only after three triggers. T1
asks for a second consumer, because "same-author second callers do not
supply independence". Both proving-ground games have the same author.

On 2026-11-05 the owner ruled that independence could come from a
specification that the owner did not write: a set of scenarios with expected
beliefs, written by a named person other than the owner and merged through
that person's own forge account. No such person was named.

On 2026-11-17 the owner replaced that route with _independence by
formalism_. Part (1) of T1 stands: a dated want for a kernel-owned reducer,
recorded in the repository of any consumer that is not the donor (decision
D8).

== Decision

=== The specification is mechanised twice, from the prose

. The contract is stated once, in prose, in `CAC-KERNEL.adoc`.
. Two _writers_ each mechanise it, one in Rocq and one in Agda. Each
  definition is a computable function, so each mechanisation yields the
  expected output of every scenario by evaluation.
. A writer receives the prose and the input packages. A writer does not
  receive the Rust implementation, any expected output, or the other
  mechanisation. A writer that meets an ambiguity records it, chooses a
  reading, and does not look for the answer.
. The two writers share no context. In practice each is a session that
  starts from the prose alone. A writer reports whether it kept the rules,
  and a breach is reported, not hidden.

=== The cross-check is byte equality of vectors

. Each mechanisation emits its expected output for each scenario in one
  canonical form: JCS (RFC 8785, ADR-0024), with the member names that the
  prose fixes.
. A gate requires the two outputs to be byte-equal, and equal to the
  vectors in `conformance/`.
. A disagreement is a finding about the prose. It is settled by changing
  the prose, and then both mechanisations. It is never settled by choosing
  the mechanisation that matches the implementation.

=== The prose must be sufficient

. Every gap that a writer reports is closed in the prose, or recorded as an
  open point with the owner's name on it.
. The corpus gains a case for each gap, so that a different reading gives a
  different vector.
. A corpus README does not state the expected result of a case. It may
  state what the case is for.

=== Chronology

Both mechanisations and their vectors for the reducer are merged before the
first pull request that designs the reducer is created. The gate reads both
times from the forge API, and never from `git log`, whose authors and dates
are self-asserted.

=== What this does not give

* Two formal models can agree and both misread the intent. The prose stays
  the owner's to accept.
* The writers are sessions of one kind of author. Their independence is
  from each other's text and from the implementation. It is not the
  independence of a second person.
* A mechanisation proves properties of its own definitions. It proves
  nothing about the Rust code. The vectors are the only bridge.

== The experiment of 2026-11-17

The slice was CAC-KERNEL section 9, "Models are compared with minds": when
a belief holds at a tick, the timeline of a model, its `until`, and
`stale_divergent`.

Each writer was given `CAC-KERNEL.adoc`, ADR-0028, ADR-0029, the README of
`conformance/tom/v1`, the corpus `policies.json`, and the seven accepted
`runner-output.json` files, in a folder that held nothing else. Each was
told not to open the repository.

The comparison with `expected.json`, both compilations and the two gates
(`scripts/check-proofs.sh`, `scripts/check-coq-assumptions.sh`) were re-run
by the author of this record. The writers' counts of gaps and their
isolation statements are their own reports.

Both files are now listed in the proof manifests, so the gates compile
them. The scripts that transcribe a package into a term and print the
vectors are not in the repository yet. Until they are, the gate checks that
the specifications compile, and not that they still give the vectors.

=== What the experiment found

. *The method works on this slice.* Two writers, working apart, reached the
  same values as the hand-written vectors.
. *The format is not specified.* The prose names the members of a segment
  and the verdict spellings. It does not fix the top-level shape (the files
  hold an object with a `models` member; both writers chose a bare array),
  the name of the id member, the member order, whether an absent `until` is
  omitted, or the shape of a refused model. Byte equality is impossible
  until the prose fixes these.
. *The README leaked answers.* The README of `conformance/tom/v1` states
  each case's verdicts in prose. Both writers used it to settle whether
  `until` is inside the lifetime. So the writers were independent of the
  implementation and of `expected.json`, and not of the README.
. *The setup had a fault.* The case `second-order` has its own
  `policies.json`, and the folder did not include it. Both writers assumed
  that the derivation takes no policy, which ADR-0028 says.
. *The writers resolved some gaps differently* with no effect on these
  seven cases. The corpus does not pin those gaps.

=== Gaps in the prose

Each needs a sentence in `CAC-KERNEL.adoc` section 9 and a corpus case.
"Pinned" means that some accepted case gives a different vector under the
other reading.

[cols="4,2",options="header"]
|===
| Gap | Pinned today

| Is the tick `until` inside the lifetime?
| By `caught` and `false-exposure-restated`, but only the README told the
  writers which reading the corpus wants.

| "Holds" means a status in one sentence and a time in another. Which one
  does "the belief of the compared kind that holds at that tick" mean?
| By four cases as one writer stated the choice; by none as the other did.

| A successor on the model's own `valid_from` gives "one segment". Its
  verdict is not stated.
| No

| "The end of the package" is not defined.
| No

| An `unknown` model whose counterpart is ambiguous: no claim, or refused?
| No

| "None supersedes the others by then": direct or transitive?
| No

| Section 9 says any superseder ends a belief; ADR-0029 says only links
  inside a key count. The scope of each sentence is not stated.
| No

| Agrees, diverges, agrees again: is the model stale-divergent?
| No

| A model of order 2 or more: does a `rejected` inner model count as "does
  not hold", like `unknown` and absent?
| No

| Belief ids are unique. The validator has the rule `belief-id-unique`;
  the prose states uniqueness only for events.
| No

| Is an order-0 belief in the output? What is the shape of a refused
  model, and is one model refused or the whole package?
| No
|===

== Consequences

=== Positive

* T1 can be scheduled. It no longer waits for a second person.
* The first result is a list of defects in the prose. That is the purpose
  of an independent reading.
* Two mechanisations exist for section 9, and both are gated.

=== Negative

* Every scenario family needs two mechanisations and a printer for each.
  The two files for this slice are about 800 and 600 lines.
* The Rocq file counts ticks in unary. It is a specification, and it
  cannot evaluate a tick near 2^53. The Agda file visits every tick.
* A third component, the printers, must be trusted to print faithfully.
  Each writer checked its transcription by a round trip to JSON.

=== Neutral

* The vectors for the reducer keep the path
  `conformance/esm/v1/independent/`, which the plan already names.
