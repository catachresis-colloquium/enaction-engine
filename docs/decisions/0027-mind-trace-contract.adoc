= Architecture Decision Record: 0027-mind-trace-contract

Date: 2026-11-14

== Status

Proposed

Extends ADR-0014 (two-rate architecture) and ADR-0016 (confidence types).
It gives a partial answer to one open problem in `CAC-KERNEL.adoc` section 8:
whether affect is state or annotation.

== Context

The contract can describe what an agent believes and why. It cannot describe
what an agent feels, what it wants, or what its body gives away. The only
affect and conation fields are two optional annotations on an epistemic
event. An event is the wrong carrier for a continuous process: affect changes
on ticks where no evidence arrives.

A designer needs to ask four questions about a mind:

. Why did the agent choose this action?
. Did it choose from the ranking, or from a commitment it already had?
. What changed its mood, and when?
. What did its body show before it acted?

ADR-0014 and ADR-0016 limit the answers. No float may cross. A utility score
may never leave the conative layer. Affect may buy attention and may never
assert a truth.

== Decision

. *A separate document.* `enaction-mind-trace/1` is a versioned document
  beside `enaction-runner-output/1`. The strict runner envelope gains no
  optional field.
. *One record for each agent on each tick.* It carries the affect token, an
  optional attention mass, the candidate ranking, the incumbent, the selected
  candidate, an optional target and the tells.
. *Rankings, not scores.* The conative layer publishes the order of its
  candidates. An order is invariant under any monotone rescale, so it leaks no
  magnitude. This keeps ADR-0016 and still explains a choice.
. *Reservoir values stay inside the host.* The contract carries the token and
  the attention mass, as ADR-0014 crossing A allows. It does not carry the
  continuous affect state.
. *A change of token has a cause.* When an agent's token differs from its
  previous token, the record names at least one event in the paired runner
  output. A token may persist without a cause.
. *Selection must be explained.* The selected candidate is first in the
  ranking, or it is the incumbent. Any other selection is refused.
. *Commitment override is derived.* A tick shows commitment override when the
  selected candidate is the incumbent and is not first in the ranking. The
  reader derives it. The producer does not assert it.
. *Vocabularies are declared.* The document declares its affect tokens,
  candidates and tells, in order. The engine owns no emotion names and no
  action names. Games and hosts own them.
. *Integers only, canonical bytes.* RFC 8785 JCS, with each integer at most
  2^53-1.

The rule names and the wire shape are normative in `CAC-KERNEL.adoc`. The
fixtures in `conformance/mind/v1` are the currency.

=== The answer to "state or annotation"

Affect is *state* inside a host and a *trace* at the contract. The event
annotation of the first slice stays, for the moment at which evidence arrives.
The trace covers the ticks between events. The contract still never carries a
continuous affect value.

== Consequences

=== Positive

* "Ill will that persists" becomes a count that a test can check.
* A host with a different affect model can conform. The contract fixes the
  form of an explanation, not the psychology.
* The validator can refuse a mind whose mood changes without a cause.

=== Negative

* A designer cannot tune reservoir constants from this document. Tuning needs
  a host-side workbench channel, which this contract does not define.
* A ranking hides how close two candidates were. A near tie and a wide gap
  look the same.
* The rule "selected is first or incumbent" assumes one commitment mechanism.
  A host that selects by another lawful rule, such as a forced reflex, has no
  way to say so yet. That is a known limit of version 1.

=== Neutral

* The event annotations `AffectAnnotation` and `ConativeAnnotation` do not
  change.
