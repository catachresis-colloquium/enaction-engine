= Architecture Decision Record: 0028-models-are-compared-with-minds

Date: 2026-11-14

== Status

Proposed

Extends ADR-0016 (confidence types) and ADR-0019 (the contract as types and
validators). It gives a partial answer to one open problem in
`CAC-KERNEL.adoc` section 8: theory-of-mind depth accounting. It is the
contract change of `docs/proposals/WEEVIL-SLICE-2-IT-MODELS-YOU.adoc`, work
package C0.

== Context

The contract already carries nested belief. In a `Belief`, `bearer` is the
agent whose belief it is, and `holders` is the ascription path, outermost
first. With `holders = ["guard-0"]` and `bearer = "contact-21"`, the belief
is guard-0's model of what contact-21 believes. The depth policy bounds the
order, and the validator refuses a package that exceeds it
(`nesting-bounded`).

Nothing said what a reader compares such a belief with. The closed-world
rule for world truth (CAC-KERNEL section 9) applied to every belief. A
reader therefore compared guard-0's model of contact-21 with the world. That
is wrong for a model. "contact-21 does not know where I am" is a claim about
contact-21's mind. Where guard-0 is in the world does not decide it.

Slice 2 needs this to be right. Its effect is an agent that holds a model of
the player's knowledge and can be wrong about it. The two false cases,
false exposure and false concealment, are the point. A reader that compares
with the world calls a false exposure right whenever the agent is in fact
where the target once saw it.

== Decision

. *World truth applies to order 0 only.* A belief of order 1 or more is a
  model. It is never compared with world truth.
. *A model is compared with a mind.* For a model with
  `holders = [h1, …, hn]` and `bearer = b`, the compared belief has
  `holders = [h2, …, hn]`, `bearer = b`, the same proposition, in the same
  package. For order 1 it is b's own belief. This is the reading of
  `enaction_trace::Trope::is_ascription_of`; the two crates agree on what
  nesting is.
. *Agrees, diverges, no claim.* A belief holds its proposition when its
  status is `certain`, `believed` or `suspected`. A model agrees when the
  compared belief holds the proposition and the model says that it does, or
  when neither holds it. It diverges otherwise. A model with status
  `rejected` says that the bearer does not hold it. A model with status
  `unknown` makes no claim.
. *At the model's tick, under supersession.* A model is compared at its
  `valid_from`. A belief holds at tick `t` when its `valid_from` is at most
  `t` and no belief that supersedes it has a `valid_from` of at most `t`.
. *Closed over stated models.* For each proposition that a model states
  about b, the package states b's compared belief when b holds it. A missing
  belief means that b does not hold the proposition.
. *Refuse what the rules cannot decide.* When more than one compared belief
  holds at the model's tick, the reader refuses with
  `model-counterpart-unique` and does not choose.
. *Derived, not asserted.* The reader derives the verdict
  (`enaction_contracts::tom::model_agreements`, in belief-id order). The
  function takes a package and no world truth. The producer does not assert
  the verdict, and no document gains a member for it.
. *No new document and no new field.* A profile that uses models declares a
  depth `max_order` of 1 or more. `enaction-validate` gains no flag.

The normative statement is in `CAC-KERNEL.adoc` section 9, "Models are
compared with minds". The fixtures in `conformance/tom/v1` are the currency.

== Consequences

=== Positive

* A designer can see four separate facts for one proposition: what the world
  is, what the target believes, what the agent thinks the target believes,
  and whether the agent is right about the target.
* False exposure and false concealment become checkable. The corpus holds a
  model that diverges in each, and a reader that compared with the world
  would get three of its four cases wrong.
* The rule is a pure function of one package. It needs no world, no clock
  and no host.

=== Negative

* A reader in another repository, such as the UMS explain lens, implements
  the same derivation in its own code. Only the corpus `conformance/tom/v1`
  keeps the two equal. A case that the corpus does not hold can differ
  between them unseen.
* The closed-world rule cannot be checked by the validator. A missing own
  belief and an own belief that the bearer does not hold look the same. A
  producer that forgets to state an own belief makes every model of it read
  as "the bearer does not hold it". The producer's own tests carry the rule.
* A model is compared at its own `valid_from` only. A model that persists
  while the target's belief changes is not compared again. A producer that
  wants the change judged must restate the model, as the `false-exposure`
  case does.
* The corpus has order 1 only, because its depth is 1. A reader that read
  the ascription path the other way round (removing the innermost holder)
  derives the same verdicts on every case in it. The crate's own tests pin
  the reading at order 2; the corpus does not.
* The rule says "holds" or "does not hold". It does not compare intervals.
  A model of `suspected` agrees with an own belief of `certain`.

=== Neutral

* The validator and `enaction-runner-output/1` do not change. A package with
  an undecidable model is still valid; only the derivation refuses it.
